Manifesto +
We built the brand for the most controversial company in technology: its language, its look, its logic. We took the founder's singular vision, translated it into something people could hold on to and feel, and made an indecipherable product impossible to ignore — on the ticker, on the tactical edge, on the pit wall, on the chalet at Davos. In our view, mystique is deliberate withholding; controversy is evidence of consequence — the same instrument, held at different angles. You have an opinion about that company. And that was the point. Most brand work is an apology: three safe directions, a committee, a palette that tests well and dies quietly. It's built to be approved at the expense of being believed; nothing that's merely approved is ever remembered. Severity is a method before it's a mood: fewer decisions, made earlier, defended harder. We bring one direction because we've already scrapped the other ninety-nine. We're a small group of designers, strategists, and filmmakers. There's no account team, no junior bench, no one to hand you off to — the people who made the brand you know are the people who will make yours. We're not interested in doing nice work; we're here to make you unavoidable, whether you're twelve people or twelve thousand. Come to us when nice stops working.

Brand designer and creative director based in London, working with technology companies, startups and the investors who back them. Sixteen years in, he measures a brand by one thing: how well it holds up once he has left the room. As Global Creative Lead at Palantir he built the brand across every platform, AIP, Foundry, Gotham and Apollo, and handed it to more than four thousand colleagues who used it every day. At FluidStack he led brand design through a period of fast growth.
